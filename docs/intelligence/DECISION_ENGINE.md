# AIP Decision Engine

## Purpose

Athlete Intelligence Platform (AIP) transforms objective testing into confident coaching decisions.

Our mission is to help coaches consistently make better decisions that improve athlete development.

AIP captures expert coaching knowledge and organizes it into a repeatable system that helps coaches lead with confidence while preserving their own coaching philosophy.

---

# Step 1 – Measure

Objective testing serves three purposes:

* Remove guesswork.
* Reveal what the eye cannot see.
* Build confidence that training is focused on the right objective.

Every assessment should provide objective information that helps guide future coaching decisions.

## Performance Outcomes

Performance outcomes measure athletic development.

Examples include:

* Sprint Time
* Jump Height
* Change of Direction

Performance outcomes answer one question:

**"Is the athlete becoming more athletic?"**

Performance testing also drives intent.

Athletes perform with greater focus, effort, and competitiveness when objective measurements provide immediate feedback.

---

## Movement Profile

Movement quality explains **why** the performance outcome occurred.

The goal is to identify the athlete's primary movement limitation.

Current movement categories include:

* Anterior Chain
* Core
* Posterior Chain

Movement assessments help identify strengths, weaknesses, movement strategies, and opportunities for improvement that may not be visible to the coach's eye.

---

# Step 2 – Diagnose

Identify the athlete's primary movement limitation.

The diagnosis should remain consistent until objective testing demonstrates that the movement profile has meaningfully changed.

The diagnosis explains **what** the athlete needs to improve—not how the coach will improve it.

---

# Step 3 – Prescribe Training Block

Training is organized into structured blocks.

Each athlete receives one primary training objective based on their current movement profile.

Examples include:

* Anterior Chain Development
* Core Development
* Posterior Chain Development
* Acceleration
* Max Velocity
* Change of Direction

Training blocks remain the primary focus until objective testing supports a change.

---

# Step 4 – Select Intervention

Choose drills, exercises, coaching cues, and programming strategies that attack the athlete's primary limitation.

Multiple interventions may accomplish the same objective.

The intervention may change throughout the training block.

The diagnosis usually does not.

This preserves coach autonomy while allowing flexibility in programming.

---

# Step 5 – Retest

Collect objective testing throughout the training block.

Evaluate whether the athlete is:

* Improving
* Plateauing
* Regressing

Retesting determines whether the current approach is producing the desired adaptation.

---

# Step 6 – Decide

Based on objective testing, determine the next coaching decision.

Possible outcomes include:

* Continue the current training block.
* Progress the athlete.
* Modify the intervention.
* Change the training block.
* Retest if additional information is needed.

AIP provides evidence-based recommendations.

The coach always maintains ownership of the final programming decision.

**AIP recommends. The coach decides.**

---

# Guiding Principles

Performance tells us **IF** training is working.

Movement quality tells us **WHAT** to train next.

Coaching determines **HOW** to create change.

Every assessment should improve the coach's understanding of the athlete.

Every assessment should also improve the athlete's understanding of their own body, strengths, weaknesses, and movement patterns.

The purpose of AIP is not to replace coaching.

The purpose of AIP is to help every coach make more confident, evidence-based decisions.
